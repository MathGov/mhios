# MHIOS v0.5 Critical-Feedback Disposition

## Purpose

This record evaluates the external critique supplied after the initial MHIOS v0.1 candidate package. It distinguishes genuine improvements from claims that were too categorical, premature, or unsupported for direct adoption. The accepted v0.2 improvements remain carried forward in the current v0.5 candidate.

## Integrated improvements

### Reflexive evidentiary discipline

MHIOS now applies MathGov claim discipline to itself. Its burden-reduction and assurance-preservation objective is recorded as a design hypothesis with dependencies, alternatives, evidence limits, and revision triggers. Artifact integrity is separated from construct validity and operational superiority.

### Normative coverage and mutation testing

The release now reports the proportion of MUST-bearing statements connected to executable checks and distinguishes direct behavioral checks from partial structural checks. Seeded mutation testing deliberately corrupts load-bearing invariants and confirms that the validator rejects the altered records.

### Specification-mass control

The 45-object catalogue remains the complete candidate model, but it is not presented as the first-build burden. Objects are classified as 12 minimal-vertical-slice core objects, 20 conditional objects, and five advanced/research objects. The initial implementation target is five screens, one Tier 2 retrospective case, one transparent calculation path, export, and replay.

### Institutional preconditions and incentive integrity

MHIOS now states that software cannot create reviewer independence, budgeted review time, non-retaliation protection, lawful authority, or consequences for misconduct. Tier 2 and Tier 3 operational claims must declare whether institutional preconditions are present, partial, absent, contested, or legally constrained.

### Automation-bias controls

For gate-material AI assistance, the reviewer must ordinarily commit an independent first pass before seeing the AI suggestion. The system records agreement, divergence, modification, and exceptions. High acceptance rates become a sampling trigger, and pilots include AI-off comparisons.

### Anti-theater Goodhart boundary

Pattern detection is treated as a challenge and human-review sampling trigger, not proof of assurance. The pilot protocol requires adversarial hollow-run testing, precision/recall reporting, pattern-aware evasion cases, and alarm-fatigue analysis.

### Reviewer-agreement study

Before Core inclusion, MHIOS calls for independent reviewer studies across repeated cases and state families, with preregistered metrics, confidence intervals, disagreement analysis, and publication of unfavorable results.

### Engineering and legal implementation integrity

The candidate standard now specifies optimistic concurrency, material-conflict handling, branch and merge semantics, evidence-freshness policies, graph-aware redaction and inference review, and legal-handling/retention boundaries. It also names the risk that discoverability or retaliation incentives can chill honest recordkeeping.

### Measurable success criteria

MHIOS now defines the rigorous-to-hollow effort ratio and hollow-run detection probability as pilot measures. Initial targets are explicitly candidate research targets rather than validated thresholds.

## Suggestions narrowed or rejected

- The complete 45-object catalogue was not deleted. Twelve objects are sufficient for the first build, but conditional objects remain necessary when rights, physical admissibility, SGP, authority, monitoring, emergency comparison, or other triggers activate.
- No universal reviewer-agreement cutoff was adopted. A single threshold such as 0.60 can be misleading across prevalence, ordinal scales, state families, and agreement statistics. The pilot must preregister interpretation rules and report multiple appropriate measures.
- The release does not claim that heavyweight frameworks always fail or that a fixed checklist length is universally optimal. These observations motivate empirical burden testing but do not establish MHIOS design rules by themselves.
- Anti-theater patterns were not removed. They were bounded as fallible sampling signals and paired with adversarial evaluation and human review.
- A clean structural test result is not treated as suspicious by itself. Reflexive review is satisfied by publishing incomplete coverage, untested construct validity, design alternatives, and adverse-test obligations rather than manufacturing a failure.

## Final disposition

The feedback produced genuine empirical, institutional, human-factors, and engineering hardening. MHIOS v0.5 is ready for a narrow working vertical slice and controlled pilot design. It is not validated, not approved for MathGov Core inclusion, and not evidence that the full 45-object or 20-screen environment is optimal.
